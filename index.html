1. INTENTIONS — What will I learn?

At the end of this lesson, I can:

  • describe a food chain and explain how energy moves through it;
  • identify producers, consumers, and decomposers;
  • read arrows correctly in a food chain; and
  • predict what may happen when one organism in a food chain changes.
Big idea: All living things need energy. In most ecosystems, energy enters through sunlight and passes from one organism to another when organisms eat.

3. EXPLORE AND INVESTIGATE — What is a food chain?

A food chain is a simple model that shows how energy and nutrients pass from one living thing to another as organisms eat and are eaten.

Example A: Grassland food chain

🌿Grass
Producer
→
🦗Grasshopper
Primary consumer
→
🐸Frog
Secondary consumer
→
🐍Snake
Higher-level consumer

Arrow meaning: The arrow points from the food to the organism that eats it. It shows the direction energy moves.

Know the roles

🌱

Producer

Usually a green plant or algae. It makes its own food using sunlight through photosynthesis.
🐇

Consumer

An organism that gets energy by eating plants or other animals. Consumers may be herbivores, carnivores, or omnivores.
🍄

Decomposer

Fungi and many bacteria break down dead organisms and wastes, returning nutrients to the environment.

Example B: Pond food chain

Producer
→
🦐Water flea
Primary consumer
→
🐟Small fish
Secondary consumer
→
🐦Kingfisher
Higher consumer

Algae use sunlight. A tiny water animal eats algae, a fish eats the tiny animal, and a kingfisher may catch the fish.

Example C: Forest food chain

🌳Leaves
Producer
→
🐛Caterpillar
Primary consumer
→
🐦Small bird
Secondary consumer
→
🦅Hawk
Higher consumer

The chain begins with a plant. Energy is transferred as each organism eats the one before it.

Why do food chains usually begin with producers?Producers such as plants and algae capture energy from sunlight and store it as chemical energy in food. Consumers depend directly or indirectly on producers for energy.
Do decomposers belong in food chains?Decomposers act on dead plants, animals, and wastes from every level. They recycle nutrients. They are often shown beside or at the end of a food-chain diagram, but nutrients—not energy—are recycled.
Does the arrow point to the predator or the prey?The arrow points to the organism receiving the energy—the eater. For example, grass → rabbit means the rabbit eats the grass.
